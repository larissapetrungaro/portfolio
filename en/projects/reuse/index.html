Reuse · An app to learn recycling and reuse

An app that teaches recycling and reuse through short lessons, challenges and a community. For my thesis, I did the research, the concept, the visual identity and the screens, and I revised the app in 2022.

Context and problem

In Brazil, each person produces a little over 1 kg of waste a day on average, and about 40% of the waste collected ends up in dumps or landfills without environmental controls. Part of the problem is information: more than half of Brazilians say they don’t understand how recycling collection works where they live.

For my Design and Visual Communication thesis, I proposed an app that teaches people to recycle, reuse and reduce waste in everyday life, with short, reliable content in one place. I did the project on my own, from research to screens.

What research showed

In the online survey, with 100 responses:

85%had never used a recycling app
74%would download an app with this content
79%wanted to learn through a game format

62% said they knew the 3 Rs (reduce, reuse and recycle), but little of that showed up in their routine. For the format, 70% chose practical how-to videos, and most spend 2 to 7 hours a day on their phone, which pointed to short content.

MethodsDesk research, online survey (100 responses), persona, analysis of 2 similar apps (Desafio Ambiental and Descartar) and SWOT analysis.

From problem to screens

The concept started from three words: education, play and awareness. I organized the content in a sitemap, built the screens on an 8-point grid and turned the findings into three decisions:

Short lessonsCourses split into lessons of about 20 minutes, with video and progress per lesson, because the audience preferred video and uses the phone in short breaks.
Play with a purposeBadges that tell a journey, from getting started to changing habits, tied to each course’s challenges. People who already know more can start at a higher level.
Learning togetherCommunities and posts to share reuse ideas and see what friends are learning.

Visual identity

I created the logo, the app icon, the category icons and the badges. The logo wraps the name in a continuous line, and the shades of green connect the app to nature. The badges follow the same 8-point grid and the app’s colors.

Solution

Before and after: the 2022 revision

Two years after the thesis, I redid the visual design, redesigned the components and created new screens, keeping the research, the concept and the brand.

Visual designThe photo background with a green overlay, which made text hard to read, gave way to a light background, labeled fields and higher-contrast buttons.
ComponentsLesson cards with progress, a new set of badges and a navigation bar with Home, Communities, Courses and Favorites.
New screensCourses with lessons and challenges, a profile with stats and a community with groups and posts.

Next steps

What I’d do nextTest the app with people from the audience, a step that was left out of the thesis, and use the results to adjust the navigation and the pace of lessons and challenges.
