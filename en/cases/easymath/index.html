EasyMath · Study path redesign

I reorganized the app’s study path into units, with a single progress indicator and a “Continue here” card, so students aged 11 to 14 always know what to study next.

Summary
My roleUX/UI Designer
TeamMe on design + 1 developer; teaching team
Duration1 year · in development
PlatformMobile app
ProblemIn the MVP, the path was a long list with textbook codes and a streak counter at zero. Students didn’t know what their next step was.
OutcomeA path reorganized into units, with a single progress indicator and proportional celebrations. Screens validated and in development.
In this case studyContextoMinha atuaçãoDecisões-chaveSoluçãoResultadoAprendizados

Context and problem

EasyMath is the companion app to a math textbook for students aged 11 to 14: animated, narrated lessons, exercises with hints, and challenges. When I joined, an MVP already existed. In it, each textbook topic is a house with 4 stages: Discover, Lessons, Apply and Demonstrate.

In the MVP, the path was one long continuous list full of textbook codes (“Ch. 7 · Bl. 2, §2.1”, “House 708”), with a zero-day streak at the top. Students could see how much was left, but didn’t know what to do now. This reading came from analyzing the MVP, since there was no usage data or student feedback yet.

My role

What I ownedMVP analysis, path and house architecture, wireframes and clickable HTML prototypes (more than 60 versions), a log of 30 decisions and the high-fidelity screens with design system v0.1.0.
What I did with the teamNaming defined with the teaching team (“Lesson” and “Discover” were kept at their request) and behavior rules agreed with the developer, who is already building the screens. I was the only designer on the project.

Key decisions

Decision 1

Organize the path into themed units, one per page

What I sawThe list kept growing. Across a whole textbook, students lost track of where they were and had to scroll a lot to find the current chapter.
Options consideredA: units with arrows. B: a row of units. C: Continue/Mastered tabs. I compared the three in a prototype with a scroll meter.
What I chose and whyA3: one unit per page, with its identity at the top (“Unit 2 of 6”) and the “‹ All units ›” bar in the thumb zone. Mastered chapters stay on the path, inside the unit itself.
Decision 2

A single progress indicator and a clear “Continue here”

What I sawThe MVP mixed textbook percentage, status circles and codes. The next step didn’t appear anywhere.
Options considered5 variations of the “Continue here” card (V1, V1a, V1b, V1c and V2) and a test of status icons vs. a 4-part ring.
What I chose and whyCard V1c, with the ring on the left, chapter, lesson title and stage. The 4-part ring (one part per house stage) became the only progress indicator, and a mastered house gets a check mark.
Decision 3

Gamification that celebrates real progress, without pressure

What I saw“0-day streak” punishes students right away. For kids aged 11 to 14, streaks and weekly goals turn into pressure.
Options consideredKeep the streak and the weekly goal, or replace them with celebrations tied to what the student has actually mastered.
What I chose and whyNo streaks and no weekly goal. Celebrations are proportional: house, chapter and unit, each with a different weight. Sound off by default, and “reduced motion” is respected.

Solution

AccessibilityWith text above 150%, the sticky button scrolls with the list. The empty parts of the ring have 3:1 contrast. Animations respect “reduced motion” and sound is off by default.

Outcome

The screens were validated with the team and are already in development. The next step, once the app is built, is testing with students. The open hypotheses are already documented for that test.

30design decisions documented and validated with the team

In devscreens being built; testing with students is the next step

Learnings

What I’d take to the next projectLogging each decision with an ID and status sped up conversations with the teaching team and the developer: what was already validated didn’t come back to the table.
