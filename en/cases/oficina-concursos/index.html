Oficina Concursos · Official website redesign

I reorganized the site around the question every public-exam candidate asks, “which exam notice is coming up?”, and brought the exam notice, the teachers and what each course offers into one place to make the purchase decision easier.

Context and problem

Oficina Concursos sells online prep courses for Brazilian public-sector exams. Visitors don’t think in terms of a “course”: they think of the exam and the position (“DETRAN-RJ, Assistant”) and want to know right away whether the exam notice is out, what it pays and whether the course covers its syllabus.

To decide, they cross-check three things: the exam notice details, who the teachers are and what the course delivers. The redesign of the official website put those three answers along the path to enrollment, each one at the moment the question comes up.

My role

What I ownedRedesign of the site’s pages: home, course catalog, a course page for each exam, teachers (list and profile), practice tests, supplementary courses and About us, on desktop and mobile. I also created the component library (lesson and FAQ accordions, course cards, exam-notice and level tags, filters) and iterated the home page through 5 versions.
What I did with the teamI was the only designer on the project, from concept to final screens. I worked with a developer who implemented the pages in the system. Exam content, prices and teachers came from the academic coordination team.

Key decisions

Decision 1

Organize the offer by exam-notice stage, not just by course name

What I sawFor public-exam candidates, the main question is “which exam notice is coming up?”. A course list without that context forces them to open page after page.
Options consideredAn alphabetical catalog, category filters, or sections by exam stage with the decision data already on the card.
What I chose and whyA catalog with search and sections by stage: Featured, Authorized, Open notices, Upcoming notices and Supplementary. Each card shows the notice status, the education level, the starting salary and the number of openings, so people can compare without clicking.
Decision 2

A course page that answers everything without leaving it

What I sawThe purchase decision depends on two things at once: the course (content, teachers, format) and the exam (openings, exam board, pay, exam date).
Options consideredA long page only about the course with a link to the notice, or two columns: the course on the left and a sticky column with the offer and the exam details on the right.
What I chose and whyTwo columns. The syllabus in an accordion, with teacher and number of lessons per subject. On the right, the offer card (“12x R$ 20.42”, what’s included) and the “About the exam” card: status, openings, exam board, fee, exam date and pay. It ends with an FAQ and a link to download the notice.
Decision 3

A home page that routes each visitor by intent

What I sawPeople arrive at very different moments: those who already know their exam, those who just want to see open notices, and those still getting to know Oficina.
Options consideredA single course showcase, or separate entry points by intent, with free content for those not ready to buy yet.
What I chose and whyA home with four entry points under “Trending courses” (Featured, Open notices, Updated, By level), the most searched courses, free materials (practice tests, live classes, answer keys) for the top of the funnel, the teachers and exam news. The current version (2.4.1) came after 5 iterations.

Solution

Each screen scrolls independently.

Also in the redesignA teachers page with filters and individual profiles (more than 20), practice tests, supplementary courses (PRF, DETRAN-RJ, TAF), About us and the mobile version of every page.

Outcome

The redesign is finished and the new site is live at oficinaconcursos.com.br. Without access to analytics, the outcome I show here is the delivered scope:

5home page versions up to 2.4.1
7exams with their own course page, on the same template
20+teacher profiles, plus catalog, practice tests and About, on desktop and mobile

Learnings

What I’d take to the next projectOrganizing the site the way candidates think (exam and notice stage) simplified the entire navigation.
